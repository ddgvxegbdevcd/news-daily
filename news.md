## Here's who Netflix just brought in to run content — and what it means for the streamer

来源：CNBC

链接：https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/16/netflix-peter-rice-chief-content-officer.html

正文长度：5555

摘要：Peter Rice brings a wealth of experience in TV and film, including scripted and unscripted shows, live sports, prestige television and theatrical distribution.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 点击展开 / 收起正文</b></summary>

> Netflix is shaking up its C-suite in an effort to fix its engagement woes.
On Thursday, the streaming giant announced that former Disney executive Peter Rice will take over as the company's chief content officer, overseeing global content organization, including scripted and unscripted series, film and live events.
Rice is replacing 10-year Netflix veteran Bela Bajaria, who most recently led the company's global content team with a focus on its live sports strategy. Bajaria was initially hired in 2016 to help the streamer launch an unscripted division as well as licensing and studio co-productions. She later transitioned to the top creative role, spearheading Netflix's $20 billion content budget.
She oversaw popular TV series like "Stranger Things," "Wednesday," "Bridgerton," "Squid Game," "Adolescence" and "Beef," as well as Netflix films like "Frankenstein" and "KPop Demon Hunters." Bajaria also helped push Netflix into live sports and events, including deals with the WWE and the NFL as well as programs like "The Roast of Tom Brady."
Prior to his stint at Disney, Rice was president of 21st Century Fox and CEO of Fox Networks Group as well as Fox's lead executive on the Hulu board. He was also the president of Fox Searchlight Pictures and helped release "Slumdog Millionaire," "Juno," "Little Miss Sunshine" and "Sideways."
The executive change follows a tumultuous period for Netflix as it faces stagnating viewership and explores avenues for growth. It comes just days after the streaming giant laid off roughly 1,000 employees, or 6% of its workforce.
The last time Netflix made cuts of this size was in 2022 when it reported its first quarterly subscriber loss in more than 10 years.
"We believe this round of layoffs reflects more structural changes to the cost base than a reaction to near-term subscriber dynamics," wrote Brian Pitz, managing director and head of internet and interactive entertainment equity research at BMO Capital Markets, in a research note published Friday.
Flagging engagement
In July, Netflix reported viewership increased just 2% in the first half of the year as the streamer suffers from a lack of shows with water-cooler-level hype.
Co-CEO Ted Sarandos admitted last month during the Bloomberg Screentime conference that the company is "not growing as fast as I want us to."
Those warnings, and now Bajaria's exit, have Wall Street bracing for more turbulence ahead.
"The sudden departure of Bajaria likely signals to us that user engagement may have remained soft throughout [the third quarter]," Pitz wrote.
Netflix is set to release fiscal third-quarter earnings on Tuesday.
In July, Nielsen reported that Netflix had generated 7.8% of TV viewership, while YouTube accounted for nearly double that, at 14.2%.
YouTube has been Netflix's biggest threat in the battle for viewers' time. In response, the streaming giant has sought to diversify its content in recent years, expanding into live sports, documentaries and video games in order to compete for share of consumer attention.
"If the opportunity is to recast [Netflix] into a broader content hub, the risk is missing the watercooler originals," Steven Cahall, equity analyst at Wells Fargo, wrote in a research note last month. "We see breakout hits as a must for the stock to work again."
What Rice brings to Netflix
Rice has a wealth of experience in television and film, most recently serving as the chairman of Walt Disney Television and chairman of general entertainment for the company. He managed a wide swath of channels across broadcast and streaming, including ABC, Disney Channel, Hulu and FX Networks. During his tenure, he oversaw the production of more than 300 shows, including "American Idol," "Modern Family," "Abbott Elementary," "The Bear," "Only Murders in the Building" and "Shogun."
He also headed up major sporting events such as coverage of the Super Bowl, NBA Finals and FIFA World Cup.
Under Rice's leadership, Disney earned roughly 300 Primetime Emmy nominations between 2021 and 2022. Of those nods, it won nearly 60 awards across its television networks and streaming platforms.
Meanwhile, Netflix's Emmy wins have slowed.
In 2021, it tied the record for most trophies won by an outlet in a single year with 44. This year, however, it only took home 16 prizes, its lowest Emmy haul in a decade.
With Rice, Netflix is also getting an executive with theatrical experience. After he was ousted by then-Disney CEO Bob Chapek in mid-2022, he transitioned into an independent producer role. Since 2023 he has produced films like "Saturday Night" and "28 Years Later." He's currently developing a movie adaptation of the video game "Elden Ring."
Late last year, Netflix struck a deal to buy Warner Bros. Discovery's film studio and streaming assets, only to be outbid months later by Paramount Skydance, which completed its acquisition of WBD in early October.
Some on Wall Street have speculated that the streamer may be interested in further M&A, including acquiring assets from NBCUniversal after it splits from parent company Comcast.
"[Rice's] long tenure at Fox, and then Disney after its acquisition of Fox, gives him unique insight into the consolidation of two major studios, relevant if Netflix chooses to go after another studio," Alicia Reese, senior vice president of equity research at Wedbush, wrote in a research note Thursday. "Even if it doesn't, Rice's experience fits well as Netflix pushes deeper into theatrical and builds a broader platform with varied content across the entertainment ecosystem."

## Nike's India campus is a long-term growth bet amid turnaround

来源：CNBC

链接：https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/16/nike-india-campus-china-sales.html

正文长度：8490

摘要：Nike announced earlier this month that it's expanding into India. The retailer has been broadly struggling, especially in the China market.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 点击展开 / 收起正文</b></summary>

> Nike is making a big bet on India.
The retailer, which has been struggling to revive its business and regain momentum under CEO Elliott Hill, is hoping the world's most populous country can help it turn the page on its performance.
As it reported its fiscal first-quarter earnings on Oct. 1, the company announced it will create a new global campus in Bengaluru, India, as part of its growth strategy. Hill wrote in a letter to employees that the new facility would be a "long-term investment" to help position Nike for success.
"India is already an important growth market and manufacturing hub for Nike, with strong capabilities and access to talent," Hill wrote.
The new campus is expected to open in the next few years and span corporate roles across the organization, including the namesake Nike brand, Jordan Brand and Converse. Nike plans to discuss its India campus in detail at its investor day next month.
In a statement to CNBC, a Nike spokesperson said expanding into India is a "natural" next step for the company as it looks to grow its global presence.
"As participation in running, basketball, football, fitness and other sports continues to grow across India, we see an opportunity to better serve athletes through stronger local capabilities, consumer insights, and innovation," the spokesperson said.
India will be the world's third-largest retail market by 2030, according to Indian conglomerate Reliance Industries. Its e-commerce market was valued at $159.25 billion in 2026 and is projected to reach $332.94 billion by 2031, according to IBEF, a business advocacy organization created by the Indian government.
Nike has endured a yearslong slump and is trying to right its business under Hill's turnaround plan. Nike also announced this month that it would lay off workers across the company as part of what it has dubbed its Pace strategy, which includes supply chain changes and reorganization of the company's geographic segments. Nike expects the effort to deliver approximately $2.5 billion in savings through fiscal 2031.
Nike's decision to invest further in India as part of its comeback comes as the sportswear company sheds revenue in another critical international market: China. Though its fiscal first-quarter North America revenue came in just above Wall Street expectations, Hill said that performance wasn't enough to offset pressures abroad.
Nike has been particularly struggling in the Greater China market, where its revenue dropped by 26% in its fiscal first quarter. It's part of what experts have said is a broader trend of American companies losing relevance and traction to domestic brands as the once-lucrative market matures.
On a call with analysts earlier this month, Hill said the company was taking "deliberate actions" to strengthen its China business, though he said those effects will take time to materialize. Hill said the company plans to refresh its physical retail, spearhead a digital cleanup and create higher quality experiences for consumers.
The Nike spokesperson told CNBC the company is "deeply committed" to China and sees the region as one of its "most important long-term growth markets," even as Nike expands its presence in India.
"China remains a long-term priority for Nike, while India represents another important opportunity to invest in the future of sport and the next generation of athletes," the spokesperson said.
Investing in a long-term vision
The company's expansion in India may be coming at both an opportune and inopportune time, according to experts. Investing in a growing international market does not guarantee higher sales — it also requires persuading Indian consumers they want the Nike brand, a challenge the company has struggled to overcome in China.
"What Nike, I think, is doing right now is going region by region and working to reestablish what the brand stands for," Simeon Siegel, a senior managing director at Guggenheim Securities, told CNBC.
Siegel said part of the dissonance for Nike is that each region and segment of the business is at a different stage of its turnaround. He said investing in India may set the company up for a more sustainable future if the China business continues to lag, but Nike needs to have a compelling product, solid storytelling and extensive distribution in the region to be successful there.
"I think the company needs to look inward and figure out what is the right way to reestablish profitability," he said. "And that involves, in certain instances, shrinking and saving and cutting, and, in certain instances, investing and looking toward the future, because the goal here is not to shrink to oblivion; it's to shrink to stability, and then ultimately growth."
The global strategy may also focus too much on a long-term vision rather than more immediate fixes, according to Jessica Ramirez, co-founder and managing director at advisory firm The Consumer Collective, who specializes in retail and apparel. Ramirez said she already thought Nike and Hill's vision were "out of touch" and the plan for the India campus didn't necessarily help its case.
"I don't know that this plan of India is an immediate solution to their problems right now," she said. "They have to plan long term, right? But they also need to plan short term, which is continuously falling apart."
She said Nike has not shared plans that are concrete enough to win back momentum in China and has to develop both regions hand in hand.
While Nike under Hill has improved its wholesale and performance categories, Ramirez said it has yet to master its direct-to-consumer and sportswear segments, which would allow the company to flourish in all of its markets.
The Nike spokesperson told CNBC the retailer is committed to investing in both countries as it tries to expand its global presence.
"Importantly, this is not an either/or decision between India and China," the spokesperson said. "We always invest market by market based on long-term opportunities to serve athletes and grow sport around the world."
A growing market opportunity
Still, India presents an attractive opportunity for many companies looking to unlock growth abroad.
Ramirez said Nike is just the latest to join a cohort of American companies looking to establish their presence in the market. While many brands began investing in China years ago, she said that trend is now pointing toward India instead, as U.S. companies see more spending pressures in the Chinese market and navigate geopolitical tensions between the two countries.
"It's to get in there for the long term and to make sure that they are there for when they can reap the positive effects," she said. "I want to say this conversation started getting a bit more engaging in the last two to three years, when companies started investing more in India."
India is one of the world's strongest market performers, driven primarily by domestic demand, investment and supply-chain diversification, according to an analysis from Accenture. With a population of over 1 billion, the country presents a growth opportunity similar to the one China offered in the early 2000s.
"It's going to be a fast-growing consumer base where we assume their income levels will continue to go up and they'll have much more purchasing power, and so maybe could we be tapping in on that market from a consumer lens," Chris Tomsovic, who leads Accenture's macro foresight team, told CNBC.
Still, one of the biggest focus points for the Indian consumer is price sensitivity, which will pose a challenge for American companies trying to break into the market, he added. Even so, many domestic brands on the market are younger, meaning there could be more opportunities right now for Western companies to take market share.
Another challenge is that the Chinese consumer has historically been receptive to American brands, whereas it will still take more data to understand how Indian shoppers feel about those businesses, he said. For sportswear retailers, the focus of the sports most popular in each region will also factor into that equation.
"Can they really localize the product, make the price point work? Can they adapt to the local social media technology ecosystem? Can they adapt to the local cultural values and norms that the consumer wants?" Tomsovic said.
"If you're able to nail that, then I think you're right. Whether you're a footwear brand, whether you're an athleisure brand, I think there's a huge opportunity there with a billion consumers," he said.

## KFC to switch to Coca-Cola beverages in U.S. restaurants for the first time in decades

来源：CNBC

链接：https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/15/kfc-coca-cola-pepsi-switch.html

正文长度：2449

摘要：KFC is switching from PepsiCo to Coca-Cola as its beverage supplier for U.S. restaurants.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 点击展开 / 收起正文</b></summary>

> KFC's U.S. restaurants will ditch PepsiCo and switch to Coca-Cola beverages next year, marking the first time in decades that the Yum Brands chain has changed its drink supplier, the restaurant company said Thursday.
Some of KFC's international markets serve Coke products, but the chain's U.S. restaurants have stuck with PepsiCo drinks ever since Pepsi bought KFC in 1986. At the time of the acquisition, KFC was Coke's second-largest fountain account, trailing only McDonald's. Pepsi later spun off KFC, along with sister chains Pizza Hut and Taco Bell — but even as the combined Yum Brands, the company stayed loyal to its previous owner. Without Pepsi, Taco Bell would not serve its iconic Mountain Dew Baja Blast.
The change comes as KFC aims to win back U.S. diners, who have ditched the fried chicken chain for rivals such as Chick-fil-A or Popeyes.
"This decision is a critical step in the Kentucky Fried Comeback," KFC U.S. President Catherine Tan-Gillespie wrote in a memo to franchisees and employees obtained by CNBC. "A new beverage offering, with a new partner in Coca-Cola, gives us a platform to innovate, meet changing tastes and give customers more reasons to choose us."
For many fast-food chains, beverages have become a critical way to attract consumers. A number of restaurants, including McDonald's and Taco Bell, have broadened their drink offerings with options such as refreshers or "dirty soda." For its part, KFC recently unveiled new drinks, including iced coffees and shakes, at Open House, its innovation restaurant in McKinney, Texas.
Coke told CNBC earlier this year that the company has helped many of its restaurant partners create new drinks.
KFC expects to be pouring Coke beverages in all of its U.S. restaurants by the end of the first quarter of 2027, Tan-Gillespie said.
Tan-Gillespie also said that Pepsi remains an important partner for KFC's parent company and the chain's international markets.
For decades, Coke and Pepsi have tussled over important restaurant partners, which represent both a sales opportunity and the chance to grow the visibility of their beverage brands.
It is unusual for large restaurant chains to switch beverage providers, but not unprecedented. In 2024, Pepsi poached Subway, the largest U.S. restaurant chain by number of locations, from Coke. Besides KFC, Coke has wooed away a number of notable Pepsi customers in recent years, including Culver's, Arby's and Panda Express.

> JetBlue's new domestic first class, BlueFirst, is now available for booking on flights across more than 30 routes, the airline said Thursday.
This option will be available on domestic flights that are not currently served by the airline's lie-flat Mint business class, which it offers on longer flights. It's part of an overhaul of the carrier's fare options, which will include basic options that don't come with things like seat assignments, a standard option, and a "flex" option, which is refundable and more easily changed.
The new first-class section comes as JetBlue seeks to return to steady profitability by growing its higher-priced offerings amid strong demand for more expensive and roomier seats.
The first flight with BlueFirst seats will be on Dec. 8, between Boston and Fort Lauderdale, Florida, followed by routes between Fort Lauderdale and New York's LaGuardia Airport, with additional flights being rolled out in the following weeks and months.
JetBlue is offering some introductory fares for BlueFirst seats for select flights, including a $299 flight from Boston to Fort Lauderdale, a $249 option from New York to Fort Lauderdale and a $299 option from New York to Liberia, Costa Rica.
The first-class tickets will offer seats that recline up to 5 inches and have up to 7 inches more legroom than Main seats, JetBlue said. Other perks include Bluetooth-connected seatback screens, food and beverage options, Group 1 boarding and expedited security.
Within the BlueFirst class, there are three tiered fare options:
- BlueFirst Base, a lower-cost option, includes one checked bag, priority check-in and security, and Group 1 boarding. Seat selection is free within 24 hours of departure, but there are fees for changes and cancellations.
- BlueFirst includes two checked bags, free seat selection, and no change or cancellation fees.
- BlueFirst Flex has the same perks as BlueFirst and additionally offers refunds if you cancel your flight ahead of the departure, with no fees for same-day switches or standby.
"We designed BlueFirst to deliver a more accessible first-class experience that combines the thoughtful hospitality and service customers expect from JetBlue with exceptional value and comfort. We're excited that customers can now start making BlueFirst part of their travel plans," JetBlue CEO Joanna Geraghty said in a news release.
