## Inflation has shoppers hunting for value. That bodes well for Black Friday

来源：CNBC

链接：https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/27/black-friday-2026-inflation-value-deals.html

正文长度：6899

摘要：Even as consumers face macroeconomic headwinds, retailers are expecting robust holiday spending through Black Friday and beyond.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 点击展开 / 收起正文</b></summary>

> Economic headwinds have made consumers more cautious — but new data and reports from retailers are painting a picture of robust holiday spending through Black Friday and beyond.
The macroeconomic environment has squeezed wallets. High inflation, fluctuating gas prices and labor uncertainty have dampened consumer sentiment and contributed to a pullback in spending. October's consumer price index rose a seasonally adjusted 0.4%, coming in higher than expected, while the personal consumption expenditures price index accelerated to a seasonally adjusted 3.8% in October compared with last year. Meanwhile, October retail sales grew at their slowest pace in nearly a year, according to the CNBC/NRF Retail Monitor.
The Conference Board's Consumer Confidence Index slid to its lowest since 2014 in September, with respondents citing concerns about inflation and the job market. It's translated into caution from some retailers like Walmart and Lowe's, which have reported slower sales, smaller baskets and drops in big-ticket and discretionary purchases.
Despite that pullback, recent earnings reports and end-of-year forecasts suggest consumers are still willing to shell out for the holidays.
Planned spending on gifts and nongifts is estimated to be $1,049 on average this year, up 5.9% from $990 last year, according to The Conference Board. Adjusting for inflation, that figure is still up 3.2% year over year.
Holiday sales are expected to top $1 trillion this year, but that growth is boosted by inflation. Though consumers have said they're willing to spend, experts have noted that shoppers are still emphasizing value and looking for deals where they can. Adobe data found that in the first 22 days of November — what it considers the beginning of the holiday season — consumers spent $82.5 billion online, marking a 9.1% year-over-year increase.
That's translating to the Black Friday and Cyber Monday week, too, as consumers take advantage of deals that started days before the traditional Friday blitz. Salesforce is predicting a record-breaking holiday week between Nov. 24 and Nov. 30, as it expects online sales to reach $357 billion globally and $84 billion in the U.S., both marking 6% year-over-year increases.
The report also found that retailers are offering bigger markdowns than last year, with discount rates up 8% in the U.S. in November.
And according to Deloitte's Black Friday survey, participation in the shopping holiday this year is expected to be the highest seen since 2016, with 87% of shoppers reporting they plan to buy during Black Friday and Cyber Monday sales.
That number is up from 82% in 2025. Consumers are expecting to spend an average of $624 this holiday, similar to last year's numbers, and Generation Z is going all in on those deals, according to Deloitte, which surveyed 1,200 consumers in mid-October. That echoes similar behavior from the cohort last year.
"It's sort of the mix of feeling the squeeze on prices, people thinking about their economic condition and their financial position, but still wanting to protect the holidays," Brian McCarthy, retail strategy lead at Deloitte, told CNBC in an interview.
He added that even as shoppers pulled back on spending over the past few months and delayed big purchases, that spend is now being more heavily concentrated into promotional windows like the holiday season. They're searching for more deals, becoming more savvy and deferring some spending.
The Deloitte data also found that 40% of Gen Z shoppers plan to use buy now, pay later for their shopping this holiday season.
"People do care about the holidays and want to make sure they can buy those gifts to share with their friends and family, but it's another way of deferring when they're going to have to pay for that item," McCarthy said.
Retailers see continued spending
On the other side of the cash register, retailers are expecting similar holiday shopping behavior and reported optimism about the consumer in earnings reports over the past two weeks.
Walmart CFO John David Rainey told CNBC last week that the company is continuing to see a resilient consumer. The retailer is leaning into value with rollbacks and deals, and Rainey said the holiday quarter was "off to a pretty good start" as macroeconomic trends start to show signs of potential improvement.
"The fact remains that payrolls are healthy, the equity markets are holding up in the face of geopolitical uncertainty, and even though consumer sentiment is somewhat lower, it hasn't translated into reduced economic output," Rainey said.
Target executives also said last week that the retailer is optimistic about holiday spending this season. The company is pricing thousands of products at $5 and under and investing in elevated products while prioritizing low prices, they said.
"We're encouraged by the start of the quarter that we've seen so far," CEO Michael Fiddelke told reporters following the company's third-quarter earnings report. "We're really excited for what guests are going to find at Target this holiday season."
Best Buy CEO Jason Bonfig told CNBC on Tuesday that while the company is seeing consumers place higher emphasis on value, those shoppers are also looking to upgrade products, sometimes into "more premium price points."
Lowe's is also expecting a solid holiday season, with CEO Marvin Ellison telling CNBC last week that the company's November sales to date are in line with its expectations. The home improvement company is also emphasizing deals for customers who may be more price-conscious, but Ellison said he's still "optimistic."
Executives at TJX Companies, a retailer known for its value proposition, said on an earnings call with analysts last week that the company is expecting sales momentum to accelerate throughout the holiday season.
"In the current environment, we believe value will be top of mind for many consumers this holiday season," CEO Ernie Herrman said on the call.
Still, TJX said warmer weather in the beginning of the fourth quarter led to a sales slowdown. The company also reported weak fourth-quarter guidance, coming in below Wall Street expectations for earnings and same-store sales. Competitor Burlington Stores also posted weak sales and provided a lower-than-expected outlook.
Gap Inc. saw a similar trend. CEO Richard Dickson told CNBC that the company's sales lagged at the beginning of the quarter due to warmer weather. The retailer posted flat comparable sales for its fiscal third quarter last week and reported sinking sales at its Athleta and Banana Republic banners. It also lowered its guidance.
But Dickson said the retailer is optimistic about colder weather beginning to drive spending again.
"We're entering the peak holiday season and selling weeks with pent-up demand and hopefully maintained good weather for shopping," Dickson said. "This could be, and would be, a source of upside."

## Microdrama apps chase profits as Hollywood moves into short-form, vertical video

来源：CNBC

链接：https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/micro-drama-apps-hollywood-vertical-video.html

正文长度：9248

摘要：The microdrama category has exploded at a time when traditional forms of media face waning interest and YouTube claims more viewing hours.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 点击展开 / 收起正文</b></summary>

> Bogdan Nesvit says his mission is simple: "We are essentially building Disney for the 21st century."
Nesvit is the co-founder of microseries app My Drama, built around the latest, bite-sized form of storytelling. Short, serialized "microdrama" videos are created specifically for mobile devices and have generated billions of views this year. They often feature romantic tropes and typically end in cliffhangers to keep viewers hooked.
The category has exploded at a time when traditional media faces waning interest and YouTube now claims more viewing hours than individual streaming platforms.
The microdrama industry is projected to reach $1.5 billion in revenue in the U.S. in 2026, according to research firm Media Partners Asia. In China, where the format originally gained popularity, microdrama revenue surpassed what was made at the box office in 2024.
"It has the potential to evolve into mainstream entertainment and basically become mobile-first video streaming of the size of Netflix," Nesvit said.
Tech companies and startups such as My Drama currently lead the category in metrics like downloads and time spent on the apps.
TikTok's PineDrama is the most popular microdrama app of 2026 so far with 13.2 million U.S. downloads through September, according to Sensor Tower data.
Startups DramaBox and ReelShort beat out entertainment giants Netflix and Disney in average mobile usage time in the U.S. in the third quarter, according to Sensor Tower.
As they see microdramas win over viewers, legacy media companies, Hollywood executives and actors increasingly want in.
Enter Fox Entertainment
Fox Entertainment was one of the earliest Hollywood investors in microdramas. The entertainment studio acquired an equity stake in Holywater Tech, the company behind My Drama, in October 2025.
Fox agreed to produce at least 200 vertical video titles over two years that would be available on the Holywater app. Thus far, hits include dark academia series "Secret Society: Till Blood Do Us Part" and "Kiss of the Pirate King." Individual series have reached tens of millions of views, according to the company.
Nesvit told CNBC in an interview that partnerships like the one between Fox and Holywater benefit studios by providing the infrastructure to publish their short-form videos and get them in front of an audience.
Beyond distributing the videos, Holywater offers advice for what types of content perform best in a short, mobile-first format, according to Nesvit.
"One of the exciting things that we learned when we started talking to studios in Hollywood around two years ago is that all of them are super hungry for consumer data," Nesvit said. "They really want to see how people are interacting with each second of the content."
Nesvit said the company will provide studios with feedback on everything from the length of a camera shot to what color dress a character should wear.
"Our goal is how we can drive audiences from YouTube, from TikTok, from Instagram to our platform, and also create those kinds of synergies [with] legacy media," he said.
Holywater recently adapted Season 3 of Fox's reality TV hit "Farmer Wants a Wife" into a microseries fit for the platform. The company also plans to create a scripted series featuring 30 three-minute episodes inspired by Fox's "Fear Factor: House of Fear."
Holywater currently relies on a combination of subscription fees, which make up about 80% of its revenue, and money from advertisers. Nesvit said the company is also exploring incorporating brand integrations. The company is already profitable, its co-founders said.
Co-founder Anatolii Kasianov said creating the videos is "very cost efficient," with an estimated expense of between $150,000 and $200,000 for an average 70-minute series.
The company's partnerships with content studios, he said, help to bring down customer acquisition costs.
"You attract organically the audience that loves that [intellectual property]," he said.
Hollywood moves in
While Fox saw collaboration as the way into the format, some longtime Hollywood executives are betting they can skip the partnerships and create microdrama platforms in-house.
Jana Winograde, Susan Rovner and Lloyd Braun, who have collectively held leadership roles at NBCUniversal, Showtime, Warner Bros. Discovery and ABC, have jointly founded a microdrama startup called aTwist.
The company, which officially launched in September, has garnered tens of millions of views as of late November across TikTok, Instagram and YouTube, where content lives in addition to the app.
"One of the specialties that we have is really what it means to be a great storyteller," Rovner told CNBC in an interview. "I was president of Warner Bros. Television. I was there for 22 years. During my time there, we had over 18 series go over 100 episodes, so I definitely think we have an expertise in getting people to care about these shows, care about the characters, invest."
"A lot of our competitors' completion rates are very low, their churn rates are very high," Rovner said. "They can often bring people into the store but they can't keep them."
The average churn rate for the most downloaded microdrama apps is nearly 60% so far in 2026, according to Sensor Tower data. The market intelligence firm notes that the number of users lost in each quarter this year has roughly equaled the number of users gained.
ATwist seeks to keep viewers enticed through high-stakes, compelling characters and unique stories. The app has already ordered second seasons of shows and puts a particular emphasis on talent, according to the co-founders.
Jesse Tyler Ferguson, known for his role as Mitchell on "Modern Family," and his husband, Justin Mikita, a Broadway producer, have collaborated on the musical series "Theater Kids vs. Zombies" for aTwist. It premiered on Oct. 15.
Other series that debuted last month included "Honey, I Think Our House Is Haunted" starring Mario Lopez and "Unboxing the Killer Doll" starring Paris Hilton.
In May, aTwist announced an exclusive partnership with Skydance's linear television channel BET to create horizontal microdramas that air first on BET and later stream in vertical format on aTwist's app.
"That brings a lot of awareness to our brand, a lot of marketing," Winograde said. "But it also gives these linear networks the opportunity to get programming at an incredibly low cost that, from what we're seeing, the audience loves."
Winograde suggested aTwist could strike similar partnerships in the future.
Media giants explore shorts
Large streaming companies, which have already faced competition from social media apps and YouTube, are exploring ways to incorporate microdramas inside their existing apps.
NBCUniversal's Peacock launched its own vertical video channel within the app this summer that features both original unscripted series and more traditional vertical dramas. NBCUniversal is also eyeing international development by investing in an Indian microdrama platform, EloElo Group's Story TV, earlier this month.
"At NBCUniversal, we see significant opportunity in mobile-first storytelling and emerging formats like microdramas to engage audiences and meet evolving viewing habits," Deepak Arya, executive vice president for corporate strategy and development at NBCUniversal, said in a press release.
Disney+, Paramount+ and Netflix similarly launched vertical channels embedded in their services this year. Notably, the channels are meant to help viewers discover traditional content and so far haven't featured original, vertical-first series.
On top of those media companies, head of Prime Video and Amazon MGM Studios Mike Hopkins told CNBC's Julia Boorstin in an interview last month that the tech company was interested in producing its own microdramas.
Challenges ahead
Despite the flurry of investment, Hollywood still faces the challenge of how to translate viewing hours into profit, especially for shorter content.
Leading U.S. microdrama app ReelShort posted a net loss of $12 million in 2025 despite bringing in total revenue of $785 million, according to research compiled by Media Partners Asia, or MPA.
The app, which was founded in 2022 by California-based Crazy Maple Studio, currently reaches about 65 million users every month, according to the company's LinkedIn profile.
However, high marketing and content costs have weighed on the company's bottom line. MPA expects ReelShort to reach profitability for the first time this year, with an estimated $40 million in net profit.
Media Partners Asia CEO Vivek Couto told CNBC that acquiring new users is the largest cost, with ReelShort spending an estimated 55% of its revenue in 2025 on marketing.
Couto also noted high content spend, especially since microdrama apps typically require a high volume of content.
"The content is very hit-driven … it wears out fast," Couto said. "The churn rate for this sector is high, so you have to fill that slate all the time, and you have to win back those users."

## Secondhand shopping is going mainstream this holiday season as consumers seek value

来源：CNBC

链接：https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/secondhand-holiday-shopping-grows-as-shoppers-seek-value.html

正文长度：5997

摘要：More consumers are shopping secondhand this holiday season as they emphasize value and uniqueness in their purchases.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 点击展开 / 收起正文</b></summary>

> As the high cost of living squeezes consumers' wallets this holiday season, more shoppers are turning to secondhand retail and thrifting.
The shift comes as consumers look for better value, buyers put more emphasis on personalization and younger generations change the way they shop. The U.S. secondhand apparel market grew 13% in 2025, nearly four times faster than the overall retail clothing market, according to GlobalData.
That segment is expected to reach nearly $80 billion by 2030, growing more than 7% annually on average, GlobalData found. Much of that expansion — 40% — was driven by Generation Z, followed closely by millennials.
"I think this has been their version of the thrill of the hunt that millennials loved during the recession days," Katherine Cullen, a global senior retail analyst at EY, told CNBC. "It's a way to really express an individual style, find something unique, something that really fits their own vibe and personality."
It goes beyond people shopping for themselves, Cullen said. With more emphasis on unique holiday gifts, shoppers are leaning more on secondhand retail than they have before, whether in clothing or in other retail categories.
They're looking for something that fits the personality of the person they're shopping for, she added.
For Vanessa Roberts, that meant finding two antique children's chairs that she says will perfectly round out her nieces' play space. Roberts, 37, said she's been thrifting for at least two decades, each time embracing the "thrill" of finding something unique or vintage.
Especially in fashion, at a time when the quality of mass-produced items has decreased, thrifted items offer more value beyond just the price tag, Roberts added.
"It's crazy that you can find better quality items at the thrift store right now from 20 years ago versus what you can find at stores right now," she said.
Roberts, who lives in Los Angeles, said she began shopping for holiday gifts in September. As she prepares her haul for December, roughly 70% of her gifting will come from secondhand stores, she estimated.
"It's been a fun little challenge, where it's like a little scavenger hunt when I go into the thrift store," she said. "I'm definitely thrifting for the holidays – I do it every year."
Emphasis on value
The trend coincides with an economy that has forced consumers to be more intentional about how they spend, as they face high inflation and elevated gas prices.
In October, the consumer price index rose a seasonally adjusted 0.4%, which was more than anticipated. Still, consumers are expected to continue spending through the holiday season, with sales projected to top $1 trillion for the first time — though some of that estimated increase is boosted by inflation.
"You have a consumer who, while they're willing to spend at the top level, they're willing to splurge selectively," Cullen said. "Consumers are looking for value, which I think is something that is making secondhand resale and re-commerce more attractive."
And while clothing is largely the entry point to buying resale, secondhand shopping is increasingly expanding into furniture, electronics and books, according to a report from consumer insights firm The Consumer Collective.
Its October report found that 34% of people surveyed said secondhand products offer better value than brand new ones.
Many secondhand items also have an inherent added value beyond the low price on the tag, according to eBay CEO Jamie Iannone. The uniqueness of those products, as well as the allure that vintage adds to them, means consumers may actually be seeking the benefits of secondhand in more ways than one.
"I can buy a product that's used, and it almost has cachet around it," Iannone told CNBC. "Consumers today, they're able to get these amazing values on products, and it's seen as very normal to be gifting pre-loved."
According to eBay's Recommerce Report in October, 70% of global consumers surveyed said they consider secondhand shopping important for managing their budgets. That same report also found that 89% of respondents plan to spend the same amount or more on secondhand products next year compared with this year.
Iannone said he thinks the interest in secondhand this holiday season is "much stronger" than last year.
"It's becoming just a more established part of consumers' shopping and gifting routines," he said.
The era of personalization
At a time when algorithms are driving shopping trends, consumers are also looking for ways to distinguish themselves and their purchases.
That's stretching beyond buying for themselves to purchasing holiday gifts for others, too.
"Secondhand is where they're finding unique items to wear," Alon Rotem, chief strategy officer at ThredUp, told CNBC. The company's holiday survey found that respondents valued uniqueness nearly as much as the value of a thrifted piece.
"To me, that represents a pretty meaningful shift in consumer taste," he added.
It's partly why ThredUp, which is an online thrift marketplace, emphasizes that each of its products is "a snowflake," Rotem said — something unique.
That's the appeal of buying preowned, rather than purchasing off the rack that hundreds of other people are reaching for, too.
"Consumer tastes have changed, and they're much more into vintage," he added. "Uniqueness has a premium in their minds."
Larger retailers are taking note, EY's Cullen said. More retailers, such as Lululemon, Patagonia and Madewell, are dabbling in preowned.
Wall Street is also increasingly watching the growing secondhand trend. Though shares of some secondhand and resale companies such as eBay and Savers Value Village have climbed this year, shares of both ThredUp and The RealReal have tumbled year to date.
"There's a lot of conversation around authenticity or the best find," Cullen said. "I think that interest and momentum, along with some of the economic factors that we're all facing, the idea that people are looking to be frugal is going to just continue."

## Retail turnarounds are taking hold just in time for the holiday season

来源：CNBC

链接：https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/retail-turnarounds-holiday-season.html

正文长度：3601

摘要：Retail earnings reports on Tuesday pointed to a mix of factors behind the strong results, including a resilient consumer and stronger execution.

<details>
<summary><b>👉 点击展开 / 收起正文</b></summary>

> Once-struggling retailers are entering the holiday quarter with improved performance and sales momentum as turnaround efforts begin to resonate with shoppers.
A handful of earnings reports on Tuesday pointed to a mix of factors behind the strong results, including a resilient consumer, stronger execution and demand for the right products at the right price.
Kohl's reported comparable sales growth of 1.2% for the period ended Oct. 31 and raised its full-year outlook as CEO Michael Bender said customers "have remained resilient" but are "migrating to value with their purchases."
He tied improvement to the company's work on product assortment, store experience and value.
Best Buy also on Tuesday posted its highest comparable sales growth in five years, at 6.1%, which prompted the electronics retailer to raise its full-year outlook.
"We have a tremendous amount of momentum," CEO Jason Bonfig told CNBC in an interview. "The fact that we saw pretty much broad-based growth across almost all of our categories is a lot of momentum that we're very excited about."
The company also credited execution, market share gains and a compelling product cycle alongside growing customer demand.
At Dick's Sporting Goods, quarterly comparable sales rose 2.6% at the core business and 0.5% at Foot Locker. CEO Lauren Hobart said "all income demographics grew" and the company "didn't see trade-down from best to better or better to good."
She similarly pointed to new products and market share gains within the category as contributing to the strong quarter. She also highlighted continued improvement at Foot Locker.
All three companies have been undergoing efforts to boost sales and get the right products on shelves to meet customer demand.
Target, likewise undergoing revitalization efforts, last week reported comparable sales growth of 5.6% in the third quarter, fueled by a 5% increase in comparable traffic. The retailer raised its full-year outlook.
CEO Michael Fiddelke at the time called the traffic gain "a healthy sign that guests are responding to our changes and choosing Target more often."
Even Gap CEO Richard Dickson told CNBC the company has moved from "rebuild mode" to "turnaround mode" at its Old Navy banner, a sign of progress, albeit not a broad-based portfolio recovery.
Michael Baker, a retail analyst at D.A. Davidson, told CNBC he sees both a resilient consumer and company-specific improvements as driving sector strength.
"Retail sales have been more resilient than most think," Baker said. His analysis showed retail sales, excluding autos and gas, rose 5.1% in the third quarter on a nonseasonally adjusted basis — the strongest quarter since the pandemic.
But he cautioned that growth appears to be coming more from transaction size than traffic, with inflation, promotions and higher units per transaction driving up spend.
Dollar Tree, which reported better-than-expected earnings on Tuesday morning, posted a 3.8% increase to traffic, accelerating from 0.4% growth in the prior quarter, suggesting more shoppers may be bargain hunting. Comparable sales at the retailer increased 4.6% during the period.
"Consumers are under pressure, and they are increasingly selective about where and how they spend," said Dollar Tree CEO Michael Creedon on the company's earnings call. "When under pressure, shoppers prioritize value and buy closer to need."
Comparable sales of everyday essentials rose 8.9% at Dollar Tree stores in the third quarter, while sales of nonessential items were essentially flat, the company said.
